## Modelling Justification
The ride-sharing database is designed around five roles: Riders, Drivers, Trips, Driver_Badges, and Driver_Badge_Awards. The primary keys were selected to provide stable and unique identifiers for each entity while supporting the relationships between the tables. `rider_id`, `driver_id`, `trip_id`, and `badge_id` are used as primary keys for Riders, Drivers, Trips, and Driver_Badges respectively. Integer identifiers are appropriate because these values are primarily used to uniquely identify records and as foreign keys in related tables. For `Driver_Badge_Awards`, I chose a composite primary key consisting of `(driver_id, badge_id)`. This prevents the same driver from being awarded the same badge more than once while avoiding the need for an additional artificial identifier. This also directly represents the many-to-many relationship between Drivers and Driver_Badges.  
  
The foreign-key constraints enforce the required relationships between the tables. Each Trip references exactly one Rider and one Driver through `rider_id` and `driver_id`. Similarly, each badge-award record references one Driver and one Driver_Badge. These constraints prevent orphaned records and ensure that trips and badge awards cannot reference nonexistent entities.  
  
The `ON DELETE` behaviors were selected according to whether deleting a parent record could destroy meaningful historical information. For `Trips.rider_id` and `Trips.driver_id`, I chose `ON DELETE RESTRICT`. A rider or driver should not be deleted when existing trips reference them because trips represent historical ride activity and financial information. Automatically deleting those trips would result in the loss of potentially important records. For `Driver_Badge_Awards.driver_id`, I chose `ON DELETE CASCADE` because an award record has no independent meaning without the driver associated with it. If a driver is permanently deleted, their associated badge-award records can therefore be removed safely. For `Driver_Badge_Awards.badge_id`, I chose `ON DELETE RESTRICT` so that a badge definition cannot be deleted while historical award records still reference it.  
  
Several business rules are enforced directly in the schema rather than being left entirely to the application. Primary keys enforce uniqueness and non-null identification. Foreign keys enforce valid relationships. Important descriptive and transactional fields, including names, contact information, trip locations, trip dates, and fare amounts, are defined as `NOT NULL` where required. Rider email addresses and driver license numbers are also candidates for `UNIQUE` constraints because they should identify only one account or driver. The `fare_amount` attribute has a `CHECK` constraint requiring it to be greater than or equal to zero, preventing invalid negative fares from entering the database.  
  
These constraints provide protection even when data is inserted or modified through different applications or interfaces. This is important for a ride-sharing platform because the database may receive frequent writes while also supporting historical and analytical queries.  

## Reflection
One design decision another designer could reasonably make differently is the choice to use `(driver_id, badge_id)` as the primary key of `Driver_Badge_Awards` instead of creating a separate `award_id` surrogate key. I chose the composite key because the main purpose of this relation is to connect a driver with a badge. A driver should normally receive a particular badge only once, so the combination of `driver_id` and `badge_id` naturally identifies an award. It also prevents duplicate driver-badge combinations directly at the database level.  
  
This choice fits the expected read and write patterns of the platform. Badge-related reads will commonly ask questions such as which badges a driver has earned or which drivers have earned a particular badge. Those queries already use `driver_id` and `badge_id`, making the composite key meaningful rather than arbitrary. Badge awards are also likely to be written relatively infrequently compared with Trips, so preventing duplicate awards at the schema level is more valuable than optimizing for extremely frequent award inserts.  
  
A different designer might choose an `award_id` to simplify references to individual award records or to accommodate future requirements such as multiple awards of the same badge. However, given the current requirements, the composite key more directly represents the relationship and avoids introducing an unnecessary attribute.
